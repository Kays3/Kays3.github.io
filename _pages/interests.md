<section class="research-photo-section" aria-labelledby="marine-biology-title">
  <div class="section-heading"><p class="eyebrow">Marine biology &amp; environmental genomics</p><h2 id="marine-biology-title">From reef organisms to genomic questions</h2></div>
  <p class="section-lead">My doctoral and field work connected coral-reef biology with questions about adaptation, symbiosis, and environmental change. During a 2021–2022 research stay with OIST’s Marine Climate Change Unit, I trained in coastal environmental-DNA sampling, filtration, research-snorkeling safety, and biodiversity field techniques. That experience still anchors computational results to the organisms, samples, and environments from which they arise.</p>

<section class="research-photo-section" aria-labelledby="ancient-intelligence-title">
  <div class="section-heading"><p class="eyebrow">Ancient intelligence &amp; population genomics</p><h2 id="ancient-intelligence-title">What ancient genomes can—and cannot—tell us</h2></div>
  <p class="section-lead">This strand of my doctoral research examined whether results from modern genome-wide association studies could be used to compare polygenic scores in ancient and present-day populations. The work also foregrounded the limits: incomplete ancient genomes, population structure, changing environments, and the uncertain portability of present-day effect estimates across ancestry and time.</p>
