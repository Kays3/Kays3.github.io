# Current Research collage

The Current Research feature uses the same editorial collage approach as Selected Writing. The copy introduces a developing research direction on transposable elements and cell states, without claiming completed TE findings.

## Assets and sources

- Generated background: images/writing/current-research-art.webp. Created with the built-in image generation tool on 25 October 2026, then compressed to WebP for the site. The microscopic and DNA imagery is conceptual artwork, not an experimental image or a regulatory diagram.
- Tissue panel: the existing images/research/digital-pathology-spatial-cover.webp, identified in the site source notes as a web crop of the user-supplied spatial T-cell and dysfunction figure using GSE263196 tissue imagery. The original asset is unaltered and opens directly.
- Embedding panel: the existing images/projects/geneformer-celltype-umap.png, showing lung cell types and disease groups in a Geneformer representation. The original asset is unaltered and opens directly.
- The tissue and embedding panels come from separate analyses and are presented as method examples. Neither is presented as a TE analysis result.
- The TE link leads to the existing public research section, rather than an unpublished working repository.
- The layout and explanations are shared across all four languages.

## Generation prompt

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: decorative background for a physician-scientist website's Current Research editorial collage. Primary request: one sophisticated tactile scientific-journal cover artwork about tissue, cell states, and transposable elements. Landscape 4:3 composition, dense visual interest toward the upper half, quieter textured ivory paper toward the lower third where genuine source figures will be overlaid separately in the website. A richly layered microscopic tissue landscape in plum, indigo and muted rose blends into expressive individual translucent cells; a flowing DNA double helix weaves across the upper right with occasional contrasting golden fragments suggesting transposable-element sequences. Conceptual molecular imagery only, no depiction of completed scientific findings or a precise regulatory mechanism. Torn archival paper edges, engraved textures, risograph grain, deep evergreen, teal, ivory and warm gold accents, matching an elegant handmade research scrapbook. Dramatic but restrained, precise editorial illustration, not a generic gradient. No text, numbers, letters, axes, charts, scatterplots, heatmaps, data marks, diagrams, logos, watermarks, faces or fabricated evidence. Do not generate a webpage or UI. Original conceptual artwork only; genuine tissue images and embeddings are separate site elements.
